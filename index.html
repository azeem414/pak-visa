Direct Route · Germany · Student visa · Pakistan

Apply for your German student visa yourself

The visa itself has one embassy fee, and no step needs an agent. Other costs, such as language tests, document attestation and the blocked account, are paid to the organisations that provide them. This guide walks through each step in order and names its source. Last reviewed 13 October 2026.

Ten steps, in order

Rules, fees and checklists change. Where a step names an amount or a document, confirm it on the embassy page before you act.

  1. Check which degree you can enter

    The Pakistani HSSC (Intermediate) is not treated as equivalent to the German Abitur, so the route depends on the level you want.

    • Bachelor's with an HSSC: either complete one year of undergraduate study at a Pakistani university in a related field, or attend a Studienkolleg in Germany and pass its entrance test (Feststellungsprüfung). The Studienkolleg needs basic German.
    • Bachelor's with A levels: you can apply directly, depending on your subjects and their number.
    • Master's: you need 16 years of education, for example a BS (Hons), B.Eng., M.A. or M.Sc.

    Source: DAAD Pakistan, Getting started

  2. Choose your language route and take the test

    The language you need depends on the programme you pick. Read the language section of the programme's own page.

    • German-taught programmes: you must pass a German exam, usually at level C1, and sometimes B2. Accepted tests include TestDaF, DSH, Goethe and telc, but each programme names its own.
    • English-taught programmes: you usually need IELTS or TOEFL. The required score is set per programme. IELTS 6.0 to 6.5 is common, and competitive programmes ask for more.
    • Some universities waive the English test if your earlier degree was taught in English. Each university sets its own policy, so ask before you pay for a test.

    Source: DAAD Pakistan, Getting started

  3. Get your documents attested in Pakistan

    • SSC and HSSC certificates: attested by the Inter Board Committee of Chairmen (IBCC)
    • Bachelor's, Master's and MPhil/MS degrees and transcripts: attested by the Higher Education Commission (HEC)
    • O and A level records: attested by the British Council Pakistan

    uni-assist asks for your school certificate with subjects and grades, your degree certificate with grades and the HEC stamps, and the minimum grade needed to pass the degree. Start early, because attestation can take weeks.

    Some sources say you also need a MOFA stamp, an apostille or an APS certificate. The DAAD Pakistan page and the embassy's document page do not list these. Ask your university or the embassy whether they apply to you before you pay for extra stamps.

    Source: DAAD Pakistan · uni-assist, Pakistan · German Embassy Islamabad, documents

  4. Get admitted to a German university

    The student visa requires admission to a state-recognised higher education institution in Germany. Apply through uni-assist if your university takes part in it, otherwise apply on the university's own website. uni-assist wants certified copies, not originals, and translations of any document that is not in English or German. It usually takes 4 to 6 weeks to evaluate your documents. Leading German universities do not process admissions through agents, according to a Minute Mirror report on German advice to Pakistani students.

    If you have no admission yet, a separate visa for seeking a place in higher education exists. It allows up to nine months in Germany, and you need a school-leaving certificate that qualifies you for German university or a Studienkolleg.

    Source: Make it in Germany, Visa for studying · Minute Mirror

  5. Prove you can pay for your stay

    For 2026 the German government asks for at least €11,904, which is €992 a month for 12 months. You can show it with a blocked account, a scholarship, or a declaration of commitment (Verpflichtungserklärung) from a person living in Germany.

    For a blocked account, you deposit the money into an account in your own name with a provider, and the provider sends you a confirmation letter for the embassy. Never send this money to a person.

    The embassy's own checklist PDFs are not all current. One still shows an old blocked-account amount of €10,236. Use only the checklist linked from the current study visa page.

    Source: Make it in Germany, Visa for studying · German Embassy Islamabad, study visa

  6. Gather your documents

    Embassy checklists for Pakistani applicants list items like these. Prepare them in the order the current checklist gives.

    • Passport, plus copies of pages 1 and 2, and any earlier visas
    • The national visa application form and a biometric photo
    • Admission letter from the university
    • Proof of funds from step 2
    • Proof of valid health insurance
    • Language proof if your programme requires it, usually level B2. Check the current checklist for which certificates and test centres are accepted
    • CV, and a motivation letter if the checklist asks for one
    • School and university certificates with transcripts, attested as in step 3. The embassy's study visa page also asks for proof of tuition payment if your course charges tuition fees. Confirm on the current checklist whether any extra stamps are required
    • Details of any earlier visa refusals

    Source: Make it in Germany · German Embassy Islamabad

  7. Register on the consular portal and wait for an appointment

    Student visa applications start on the Federal Foreign Office's Consular Services Portal. The Islamabad embassy uses a waiting list: you register, and the mission allocates appointments later, in order of registration, as capacity allows. Only complete and correct data receives an appointment.

    This guide covers the German Embassy in Islamabad. It handles applicants from Islamabad Capital Territory, Gilgit-Baltistan, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Azad Jammu and Kashmir, and Punjab. If you live in Sindh or Baluchistan, your application goes to the consulate general in Karachi, so this guide does not apply to you. Do not email or phone to ask about your place in the queue, because the embassy asks applicants not to.

    Open the Consular Services Portal

    Source: German Embassy Islamabad, study visa · Make it in Germany

  8. Attend your appointment and pay the fee

    Visa applications can only be submitted in person. The national visa fee is €75. It is paid in cash in Pakistani rupees at the current exchange rate, so confirm the rupee amount on the day of your appointment.

    The embassy charges no other fee. If you apply through an official external service provider, that provider adds a service fee. Nobody can pay extra to improve the outcome.

    Source: German Embassy Islamabad, general visa information

  9. Wait for the decision

    The embassy gives five to ten weeks for student and work visa applications, depending on the category. When a decision is made, you are told to submit your passport for the visa. Check that your name, passport number and photo on the visa are correct.

    Source: German Embassy Islamabad, general visa information

  10. After you arrive in Germany

    • Register your home address at the residents' registration office
    • Book an appointment with the local foreigners authority and apply for your residence permit before the entry visa expires
    • Take out new health insurance in Germany
    • Residence permits for study are usually issued for two years first, and can be renewed for two more if you have not finished
    • You may work up to 140 full days or 280 half days a year, or up to 20 hours a week

    Source: Make it in Germany, visa overview (Jan 2024) · Make it in Germany, Visa for studying
